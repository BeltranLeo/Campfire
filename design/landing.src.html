Who goes first?

Trading with a stranger, someone has to go first. Either you send the money and hope, or you hand your coins to a platform and trust it. Campfire changes who goes first.

The usual way

A middleman holds your coins.

A platform's walletFrozen
The other person

A contract goes first.

The seller locks the coins in a contract anyone can inspect. It can only pay the buyer or give them back to the seller. Nobody else — not even Campfire — can take them.

Inside the contract

How does it work?

You

A seller and a buyer.

One has Bitcoin Cash, the other has fiat money. Each signs in with a compatible Bitcoin Cash wallet. Between them, a space for something they can both trust.

The contract

The coins go into a contract.

Each trade creates its own contract on the Bitcoin Cash network. The seller locks the coins there, and from then on only the contract's rules can move them.

The network

The network verifies every move.

Your browser rebuilds the contract and checks the deposit on its own, without trusting Campfire. Every move is a real transaction anyone can look up.

The arbiter

If something goes wrong, someone mediates.

Either person can open a dispute. An arbiter named in the offer decides who gets the coins: the buyer or the seller. The arbiter can never keep them.

Four ways out

Every possible ending is written in the code.

  • Release → buyerThe seller signs. The normal ending.
  • Refund → sellerThe buyer signs. For when they won't pay.
  • ArbitrateThe arbiter's verdict pays the buyer or the seller. Nobody else.
  • Reclaim after 14 daysIf everyone else disappears, the seller takes the coins back alone.
Technical details
  • CashScript contract, one per trade, with exactly these four spending paths. Source on GitHub.
  • 0-conf with DSProofs: after 5 quiet seconds without a double-spend proof, the deposit is safe to act on.
  • New keys per trade: each trade uses a fresh address of your wallet, so trades aren't linked on-chain.
  • Encrypted payment details: revealed in the browser only to the other person in the trade.
  • Transparency page: every contract exit, with its transaction.

Questions?

Still unsure? Start with the demo trade: real BCH, real contract, and no money of yours moves.

Do I need Bitcoin Cash to buy?
No. To buy you only need a compatible Bitcoin Cash wallet to receive the coins. You pay the seller in fiat money.
Do I need to create an account?
No. Connecting your wallet is the only sign-in. There are no accounts, passwords or e-mail addresses.
How do I pay or get paid?
Each offer lists the payment methods the seller accepts — for example a bank transfer or cash in person. The payment happens outside Campfire, directly between the two of you.
Who holds the coins during a trade?
A contract on the Bitcoin Cash network. It can only pay the buyer or return the coins to the seller. Campfire cannot move them.
What if the buyer doesn't pay in time?
The seller sets the time to pay. If it runs out, the trade is cancelled and the coins go back to the seller. Either side can also open a dispute.
What if the other person disappears?
Either of you can open a dispute, and an arbiter named in the offer decides who gets the coins. The arbiter can never keep them. And if everyone disappears, after 14 days the seller can take the coins back alone.
When does my wallet ask me to sign?
Only when coins move: publishing a sell offer, locking coins, releasing them, or cancelling. Buttons that open your wallet say so.
How much does it cost?
There are no trading fees today. Publishing an offer costs less than a cent in network fees, and sometimes Campfire pays it for you. Fees for the price oracle and the arbiter are planned for the future.
What does “Simulated” mean?
In demo trades the bank payment is not real. Everything marked “On-chain” is a real transaction you can check.
Can I trade PUSD?
Not yet. PUSD is a dollar token on Bitcoin Cash (1 PUSD stays close to 1 US dollar). Offers in PUSD are coming soon.
